# Narration — Drug Model explainer

One block per scene. Start/end come from `index.base.html` (`data-s` / `data-e`); `retime.py` stretches scenes to fit.
Wording follows the SNOMED CT Drug Model Implementation Guide (docs.snomed.org).

| # | Time | Budget | Narration |
|---|------|--------|-----------|
| 1 | 0.5–6 | ~12 | The SNOMED CT Drug Model: one common language for medicines, shared around the world. |
| 2 | 6.3–13 | ~16 | Every jurisdiction has its own market: authorized product names, packaging, national regulators. But clinical meaning must be shared across jurisdictions. |
| 3 | 13.3–23 | ~22 | The guide describes a two-layer model. The International Drug Model is the clinical foundation. National Drug Extensions provide the real-world representation. |
| 4 | 23.3–37 | ~30 | Internationally, medicines are defined by active ingredient, dose form and strength: Medicinal Product, Medicinal Product Form, and Clinical Drug — independent of brand and packaging. |
| 5 | 37.3–49 | ~26 | Each National Release Center builds a drug extension on top: Real Clinical Drugs, Clinical Drug Packages, and Real Clinical Drug Packages — the products authorized in its market. |
| 6 | 49.3–58 | ~16 | Together they form a single, integrated structure, where extension concepts build directly on international concepts. |
| 7 | 58.3–68 | ~22 | So a brand in one country and a brand in another share the same Clinical Drug. That is interoperability across jurisdictions. |
| 8 | 68.3–78 | ~22 | Extensions align with the International Edition, reuse international concepts wherever possible, and are harmonized through the Drug Extension User Group. |
| 9 | 78.3–84 | ~12 | One international foundation, many national extensions: interoperable medicines. Learn more at docs.snomed.org. |
