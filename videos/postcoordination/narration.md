# Narration draft

One block per scene. Start/end come from `index.html` (`data-s` / `data-e`).
Word budget assumes ~2.4 words/second so each line fits its scene with a little breathing room.

| # | Time | Budget | Narration |
|---|------|--------|-----------|
| 1 | 0.5–6 | ~12 | Postcoordination: saying more with SNOMED CT, without a new code for every detail. |
| 2 | 6.3–13 | ~16 | Clinical detail is almost endless — left, open, spiral, healing. A concept for every combination isn't realistic. |
| 3 | 13.3–22 | ~21 | A precoordinated expression uses a single concept. Postcoordination combines several concepts, following SNOMED CT rules — no new concept needed. |
| 4 | 22.3–36 | ~32 | Take "fracture of the left tibia". Start with a focus concept: fracture of tibia. Then refine it: the finding site is the tibia — and that value is refined again, with laterality: left. |
| 5 | 36.3–44 | ~18 | Read the symbols: a colon starts the refinement, equals links an attribute to its value, and braces group attributes that belong together. |
| 6 | 44.3–52 | ~18 | Another example: acute bacterial pneumonia — bacterial pneumonia, refined by its clinical course. One expression. Precise meaning. Computable. |
| 7 | 52.3–61 | ~21 | Why use it? To capture new meanings, exchange data between systems — including FHIR — map other code systems, and power language processing. |
